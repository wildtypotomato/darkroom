# Design Philosophy — Universal Rules

The floor under every Darkroom artefact. A rule earns its place only if it surfaces in three or more of our four sources, or sits beneath all of them as bedrock. Each rule lands on **both** the PDF poster and the 9:16 video — if it does not, it does not belong here.

Voice: Vignelli's absolutism, Lupton's precision, Refactoring UI's pragmatism, Caldwell/Zappaterra's pacing. Read it as a checklist, not an essay.

---

## Rule 1 — The grid is the contract

**Statement.** Every element snaps to a grid. No floating, no eyeballed placement, no "looks about right".

**Why.** The grid is the proportional regulator that turns arbitrary placement into authority. Vignelli calls it "the basic structure" (Canon p. 40). Lupton calls it "a flexible and resilient skeleton" (TwT p. 151). Refactoring UI codifies it as 8/16 px units (RUI Rule 3). Caldwell/Zappaterra build entire chapters on proportional column grids (Editorial Design pp. 121–140).

**Sources.** All four.

**PDF poster.** A3 portrait → 6-column multicolumn grid, 18 mm outer margins, 22 mm top, 25 mm bottom (asymmetric, per Lupton p. 179). Baseline grid equal to body leading (e.g. 13 pt for 10/13 body). Image bottoms snap to the baseline. Section starts hang from a fixed datum at roughly the upper third.

**9:16 video.** 1080 × 1920 → single-column grid with 80 px side margins, 6-row horizontal grid. Title datum y = 640 px. Subtitle band y = 1500 px. End-card logo row 6 centered. The same datum holds across every shot — text never floats to a new vertical.

## Rule 2 — Size is the first lever; color is the last

**Statement.** Build hierarchy with scale and weight. Reach for color only after scale has done its work.

**Why.** The eye reads size before everything else (RUI Rule 1). Lupton: "Decisive jumps read as confidence; tiny jumps read as indecision" (TwT p. 42). Vignelli treats scale as "the most relevant detail" (Canon p. 74). Caldwell/Zappaterra demand 3–4 visible levels of size contrast on every spread (Editorial Design Rule 3).

**Sources.** All four.

**PDF poster.** Use a 1.5–2× ratio between hierarchy levels. Body 10 pt → subhead 18 pt → section header 32 pt → title 80 pt. No mid-sizes. Headline at 80–120 pt minimum on A3 — large enough to read at three metres.

**9:16 video.** Title 200 pt, body 56 pt, caption 36 pt. Three levels, big gaps. The size jump is the keyframe — no zoom transition needed. Title readable at arm's length on a phone (≥48 px floor for any subtitle; chapter title ≥96 px).

---

## Rule 3 — Two typefaces. Maximum.

**Statement.** One family for body, one contrasting partner for display. Never three.

**Why.** Vignelli: "Out of thousands of typefaces, all we need are a few basic ones" (Canon p. 55). Lupton: "Combining typefaces is like making a salad — strive for contrast rather than harmony" (TwT p. 54). Refactoring UI: "Most interfaces only need one font well-executed" (RUI Rule 6). Caldwell/Zappaterra: "Avoid more than 2–3 typefaces" (Editorial Design Rule 9).

**Sources.** All four.

**PDF poster.** Pair a serif text family (Sabon, Miller, Garamond Premiere Pro) with a contrasting sans display (Futura, Verlag, Gill). No third family. No italic-as-third-voice — italic lives inside the body family.

**9:16 video.** Same pair. Display cut for the chapter card; caption cut for burned-in subtitles (sturdier strokes survive video re-encoding, per Lupton p. 41). One family on screen at a time when possible.

---

## Rule 4 — De-emphasise to emphasise

**Statement.** Do not make the important thing louder. Make everything else quieter.

**Why.** Wathan and Schoger's central inversion (RUI Rule 2). Lupton echoes it: "Emphasis can be created with just one shift" (TwT p. 132). Caldwell/Zappaterra build dominance through contrast, not addition (Editorial Design Rule 3). Vignelli: "In a world where everybody screams, silence is noticeable" (Canon p. 72).

**Sources.** Three (RUI, Lupton, Editorial Design); Vignelli supplies the moral.

**PDF poster.** Headline at 100% black. Supporting copy at L60–70% grey, smaller, fewer words. Pull quote earns colour because everything else has surrendered it.

**9:16 video.** Background photo fades to 30–40% opacity. Foreground text at full white. Subtitle at 50% grey. The text dominates because the surroundings retreat.

---

## Rule 5 — White space is structural

**Statement.** Negative space is part of the grid. Reserve at least 15–40% of the surface and never let content fill it.

**Why.** Vignelli: "White space is the correspondent of space in architecture… it is really the white that makes the black sing" (Canon p. 92). Lupton frames margins as "the user interface of the book" (TwT p. 153). Caldwell/Zappaterra: "Negative space is structural, not decorative" (Editorial Design Rule 5). Refactoring UI: whitespace is "active design, not leftover" (RUI Rule 3).

**Sources.** All four.

**PDF poster.** 15 mm minimum outer margin, more on the outside edge. 18–24 pt of vertical space between headline and body. The poster should look ⅓ empty when squinted at — that emptiness is where authority lives.

**9:16 video.** 15–20% of frame width left empty on the active text side. Bottom 200 px reserved for subtitle band. Top 80 px reserved for chapter label. Body content lives in the middle ~1500 px — never bleeding to the edge.

---

## Rule 6 — One ink, one accent. Earn every additional colour.

**Statement.** Black or near-black on warm/off white, plus one signature colour used as an identifier — never as mood.

**Why.** Vignelli demands primary palette only, used as signifier (Canon Rule 7). Lupton uses one near-black plus one brick-red across her entire book (TwT Rule 1). Refactoring UI: muted base, one fully-saturated accent (RUI Rule 10). Caldwell/Zappaterra: "One bold accent for cross-heads, pull quotes, visual punctuation" (Editorial Design palette).

**Sources.** All four.

**PDF poster.** Body in #1A1A1A on #FAF7F2. One accent (brick-red, deep navy, or burnt sienna) for section labels, drop caps, or rules. Captions at 70% black. No gradients. No tints sneaking in as a third colour.

**9:16 video.** Same near-black + accent across all scenes. Accent reserved for the chapter card and end card. Subtitles white on a 60%-opacity black bar (scrim guarantees 4.5:1 contrast — RUI Rule 5).

---

## Rule 7 — Alignment is a typographic act

**Statement.** Default to flush-left, ragged-right. Never justify a column under 60 characters. Never centre body copy.

**Why.** Vignelli: "Most of the time we use flush left… justified is fundamentally contrived" (Canon p. 66). Lupton: "Choosing alignment is a fundamental typographic act" (TwT p. 112). Refactoring UI baselines flush-left as the default for all body text. All three reject justification on short measures; all three reject centred body.

**Sources.** Three (Vignelli, Lupton, RUI); Editorial Design implicitly enforces this through column rhythm.

**PDF poster.** Body flush-left, 45–75 characters per line. Title flush-left or — only if symmetrical and ceremonial — centred. Captions flush-left, italic, never centred.

**9:16 video.** Subtitles centred or flush-left. Never justified (justification fails at short video measures, every time). Chapter titles flush-left for editorial feel; centred only for end-card / ceremonial close.

---

## Rule 8 — Captions are sentences, not labels

**Statement.** A caption extends the story. It must read independently of the image and the body.

**Why.** Lupton: "Captions are the most-read text on a magazine page" (TwT p. 130). Caldwell/Zappaterra: "A caption extends the story… deserves craft, voice, and completeness" (Editorial Design Rule 4). Vignelli's semantics rule (Canon p. 10) demands every element carry meaning — a label-caption fails that test.

**Sources.** Three (Lupton, Editorial Design, Vignelli by extension).

**PDF poster.** Captions in italic 7.5/9.5 pt, placed adjacent to the image. Lead with a small-caps tag ("AUTUMN, 2025") then a written line: "Designer Sarah Chen works from her Brooklyn studio, where natural light and an analog table inform her image selection."

**9:16 video.** Caption card on screen ≥ 2.5 s. Text written, not described: "This process — selecting light, framing subjects — takes time. It cannot be rushed." Never "A photograph of the designer at work."

---

## Rule 9 — One signal of emphasis is enough

**Statement.** Italic, OR a weight change, OR a colour shift. Never two. Never three.

**Why.** Lupton's headline crime: "BOLD, *ITALIC,* UNDERLINED CAPS!" (TwT p. 132). Caldwell/Zappaterra: hierarchy is built through contrast, not addition (Editorial Design Rule 3). Refactoring UI: "Never use more than 3 font weights in a single interface" (RUI anti-slop).

**Sources.** Three.

**PDF poster.** Emphasis = italic in body; weight change for headers; size jump for the title. Never combine bold + italic + colour on the same word.

**9:16 video.** Emphasis = a single italic word in a caption, OR a colour shift, never both. Animated emphasis (size pulse, weight pulse) counts as a signal — one per shot.

---

## Rule 10 — Reject decoration. Constraint carries personality.

**Statement.** No drop shadows used decoratively. No gradients. No bevels. No "trendy" transitions. No purple-blue Spotify-Wrapped gradient. Personality lives in font choice, palette discipline, border radius, word choice — not in effects.

**Why.** Vignelli's anti-vulgarity stance: "Vulgarity implies a blatant intention of a form of expression that purposely ignores any form of established culture" (Canon p. 14). Refactoring UI: "Personality comes from constraint, not decoration" (RUI Rule 12). Lupton's twenty named type crimes (TwT pp. 48–214). Caldwell/Zappaterra: "Avoid decorative elements that don't serve hierarchy" (Editorial Design preset).

**Sources.** All four.

**PDF poster.** No bevels, no gradient fills, no glow, no faux-3D. Shadows used only when they earn semantic weight (e.g. lifting a card 2–4 pt to indicate elevation, with a consistent top-left light source — RUI Rule 8).

**9:16 video.** No lens flares. No bloom. No background motion-graphic loops. No "swipe-bounce" transitions. Clean cuts. Typography that stays still or moves along a single grid axis. Motion must be purposeful — revealing information, not performing motion.

---

## The floor

If a Darkroom artefact violates any of these ten rules, it is not finished. The CRITIQUE stage tests against this list before delivery. The anti-slop gallery (`anti_slop.md`) names the specific failure modes; this file holds the principles those failures contradict.
